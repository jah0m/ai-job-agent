# Practice assessment

This is an original coaching rubric, not a validated selection instrument.
State the applicable dimensions at the beginning; keep them consistent within
the session. For a narrowly focused drill, assess only relevant dimensions.

## Dimensions

- **Relevance:** answers the actual question and addresses the target role.
- **Evidence and ownership:** specific experience, clear personal actions,
  supported outcomes, and consistency with supplied materials.
- **Reasoning and depth:** explains why, alternatives, tradeoffs, and learning;
  technical accuracy only when relevant and supportable. Verify uncertain
  technical claims against primary documentation before calling them errors.
- **Communication:** understandable structure, appropriate detail, and responses
  to clarification. Language expression feedback stays separate from technical
  competence; do not infer delivery traits not observable in the input.
- **Career/role understanding:** coherent motivations and informed two-way fit
  questions, grounded in available company/role facts.

Use integers 1–5 per observed dimension, with these shared anchors:

1. Answer provides little relevant support or remains substantially unclear.
2. Partly relevant; important reasoning or concrete evidence is still missing.
3. Clear, relevant answer with a specific example and adequate explanation.
4. Strong support and clear ownership; follows through on probing questions.
5. Precise, well-supported explanation that also addresses limits, alternatives,
   or transfer to the target context where relevant.

Every score needs a question ID, an answer excerpt or labeled paraphrase, and a
reason. Mark unobserved dimensions **N/A**, not 0. A missing company brief may
make company-specific motivation unassessable; do not grade invented knowledge.
Do not punish a truthful boundary or missing confidential details.

## Final review contract

Include:

1. Practice label, mode, target, main questions answered/skipped, and limitations.
2. Per-question feedback: what was convincing, what was unclear, and one concrete
   next change. Reference answer IDs. Separate original and assisted attempts.
3. Applicable dimension scores with evidence and unassessed dimensions.
4. A concise overall qualitative assessment of this session's demonstrated
   answers, not the person's employability or likelihood of receiving an offer.
5. Up to three prioritized exercises, each with an observable success criterion,
   and one proposed question for the next session.

Do not output a percentage or aggregate by default. If an aggregate is requested,
use the arithmetic mean of assessed dimension scores on the 1–5 scale, state
the denominator and excluded dimensions, and label it a training summary.
Never present it as a pass probability or employer benchmark.

In coaching mode, default scores describe initial unaided attempts. Discuss
retry improvements separately; assistant-written answers are never candidate
performance. An early stop produces a partial review, not a fabricated full one.
Compare sessions only when role, mode, scope, and rubric are sufficiently aligned;
otherwise describe qualitative changes without claiming a numerical trend.
